# Mosque Architecture Patterns — Reference for Fact-Checking

Canonical styles used in MosqueList. Use this when assigning or verifying `architecturalStyle` in mosque data. Sources: Encyclopaedia of Islam, Britannica, Wikipedia (cited), regional scholarship.

---

## 1. Ottoman

**Scope:** Mosques built under or influenced by the Ottoman Empire (c. 14th–20th century), especially in Turkey, Balkans, Levant, North Africa.

**Features:** Central dome, semi-domes, pencil minarets, often large courtyard; tilework (Iznik), calligraphy; hypostyle or central-dome plan.

**Examples:** Sultan Ahmed (Blue Mosque), Süleymaniye, Selimiye; Imperial Mosque (Pristina).

**Notes:** “Classical Ottoman” = high period (16th–17th c.); “Ottoman Revival” = 19th–20th c. revival.

---

## 2. Mughal

**Scope:** Indian subcontinent under Mughal rule (16th–19th century); also later buildings in that tradition.

**Features:** Bulbous domes, red sandstone and white marble, chattris, iwans, extensive inlay and pietra dura; large courtyards, gateways.

**Examples:** Jama Masjid Delhi, Badshahi, Taj-ul-Masajid, Shah Jahan Mosque (Thatta).

---

## 3. Umayyad

**Scope:** Early Islamic period (7th–8th century), Umayyad Caliphate (Damascus-centered).

**Features:** Hypostyle hall, arcades, courtyard (sahn), minaret (often square tower), mosaic and stone; influence from Byzantine and Sassanian.

**Examples:** Umayyad Mosque (Damascus), Al-Aqsa (rebuilt/expanded under Umayyads), Dome of the Rock complex.

---

## 4. Persian / Persian-Islamic

**Scope:** Iran and Persian-influenced regions (Central Asia, parts of South Asia).

**Features:** Iwans, vaulting, turquoise and blue tile (kashi-kari), muqarnas, double-shell domes; four-iwan plan.

**Examples:** Imam Reza Shrine, Jamkaran; many Iranian and Central Asian shrines.

---

## 5. Persian-Timurid / Timurid

**Scope:** 14th–16th century, Timur and successors (Samarkand, Herat, Bukhara, etc.).

**Features:** Large scale, tile mosaic, ribbed domes, high portals (pishtaq), geometric and floral patterns.

**Examples:** Kalon Mosque (Bukhara), Bibi-Khanym; often grouped with “Persian” in our data.

---

## 6. Fatimid

**Scope:** North Africa and Egypt under Fatimid Caliphate (10th–12th century).

**Features:** Stucco decoration, keel arches, domes over mihrab area; influence on later Egyptian and Maghrebi mosques.

**Examples:** Al-Azhar (founded Fatimid, later expanded), Al-Hakim; some Tunisian and Algerian heritage.

---

## 7. Mamluk

**Scope:** Egypt and Syria under Mamluk Sultanate (13th–16th century).

**Features:** Stone construction, ablaq (alternating stone), muqarnas, multiple domes and minarets; madrasa-mosque complexes.

**Examples:** Many Cairene mosques; “Mamluk Revival” = 19th–20th c. revival of this style.

---

## 8. Moorish / Maghrebi

**Scope:** North Africa and Andalusia (Spain); also “Moorish” revivals elsewhere.

**Features:** Horseshoe and multifoil arches, zellige tile, stucco, courtyards with fountains; minarets often square.

**Examples:** Hassan II (modern with Moorish elements), Qarawiyyin; “Moorish” often used for fusion (e.g. Sheikh Zayed).

---

## 9. Sudano-Sahelian

**Scope:** West Africa (Mali, Senegal, Burkina Faso, etc.), traditional and modern.

**Features:** Mud/adobe construction, wooden projections (toron), often flat roof; conical or pyramidal minarets in some regions.

**Examples:** Great Mosque of Djenné, Great Mosque of Bobo-Dioulasso.

---

## 10. Modern Islamic / Contemporary Islamic

**Scope:** 20th–21st century mosques using modern materials and plans while retaining Islamic elements (domes, minarets, calligraphy, geometry).

**Features:** Concrete, steel, glass; simplified domes and minarets; sometimes “fusion” with local or historic styles.

**Examples:** Many national mosques (Putra, Istiqlal, Faisal, etc.); “Modern Islamic,” “Contemporary Islamic,” “Modernist Islamic” used in our data.

---

## 11. Malay / Southeast Asian

**Scope:** Malaysia, Indonesia, Brunei; local traditions plus Persian, Indian, and colonial influences.

**Features:** Tiered roofs, wooden structures in traditional forms; modern builds often add domes and minarets.

**Examples:** National Mosque Malaysia, many Indonesian grand mosques; we sometimes use “Malay and Persian” or “Modern Islamic” for these.

---

## 12. Generic “Islamic”

**When to use:** When a mosque does not clearly fit one regional/historical style, or when sources only say “Islamic” or “traditional Islamic.” Prefer a more specific style when sources support it.

---

## Fact-check checklist for architecturalStyle

- [ ] Style matches region and period of construction (e.g. Ottoman for Ottoman-era Turkey/Balkans).
- [ ] If “Islamic” only, confirm no clearer style in Britannica / Encyclopaedia of Islam / reliable regional source.
- [ ] Fusion styles (e.g. “Moorish, Mughal, Ottoman fusion”) are used only when the building explicitly blends multiple traditions.
- [ ] Avoid inventing styles; use terms from this list or add a new one only with a cited source and a short note in this doc.
