# Audience Personas

Who MosqueList is for and how to speak to each group. Use this with [content-strategy.md](./content-strategy.md) to shape tone, depth, and topics for the product and blogs.

---

## 1. Muslims (Ummah-focused)

### Who they are
- Practicing Muslims (any age, global) who care about visiting holy sites, planning Umrah/Hajj, or building a “mosque bucket list.”
- May prioritize the three holiest sites, then other significant mosques; value correctness of Islamic terms, dates, and significance.
- Often search in English plus Arabic or local language; some want both spiritual context and practical info (visiting hours, dress code).

### What they want
- **Accuracy** – Correct names (English/Arabic), significance (e.g. “third holiest”), and respectful handling of Quran/hadith if cited.
- **Discovery** – Find mosques by country, capacity, “holy site,” and “tourist friendly”; save favorites and track visits.
- **Inspiration** – “Where to pray next”; history and significance; connection to faith and heritage.
- **Practical** – Links or hints to visitor info, prayer times, and travel (without replacing specialized Hajj/Umrah services).

### How we appeal
- Use reverent, accurate language (e.g. “Masjid al-Haram,” “Prophet Muhammad ﷺ”); avoid casual or sensational tone.
- Highlight “Holy Site” and significance; offer filters for holy sites and women’s prayer areas.
- Content: sacred mosques, history of grand mosques, Quran/hadith context where relevant; visitor tips for holy sites.
- Ensure mosque data and any scripture references follow [skills.md](./skills.md) so trust is maintained.

### Example messaging
- “Your journey to the sacred places begins here.”
- “Track the mosques you’ve visited and plan your next destination.”
- “From the three holiest sites to architectural gems—discover, save, and visit.”

## 2. Muslim travelers & diaspora

### Who they are
- Muslims living or traveling abroad who want to find a mosque in a new city or country, or plan trips around visiting famous mosques.
- May care about women’s prayer space, language, and halal-friendly areas; often use “biggest mosque in [country]” or “mosques in [city].”

### What they want
- **Discovery by place** – Country/city filters; “biggest in this country”; map or list of notable mosques.
- **Practical** – Tourist-friendly, visiting hours, dress code, women’s area; combine spiritual value with trip planning.
- **Bucket list** – Save favorites and “visited” status; share lists with family or friends.

### How we appeal
- Strong filters (country, tourist-friendly, women’s area); clear “Tourist Friendly” and “Women’s Prayer Area” badges.
- Content: “Biggest mosque in [country],” “Best mosques to visit in [region],” visitor guides.
- Tone: welcoming and practical; still respectful of sacredness.

### Example messaging
- “Find the world’s most magnificent mosques—filter by country, capacity, and visitor access.”
- “Plan your next trip: discover tourist-friendly mosques and track your visits.”

## 3. Tourists & cultural travelers (non-Muslim)

### Who they are
- Curious travelers who visit mosques as cultural and architectural landmarks (e.g. Blue Mosque, Sheikh Zayed, Hassan II).
- May not know Islamic terms; want “can I visit,” dress code, opening hours, and brief, neutral context (history, architecture).

### What they want
- **Access** – Which mosques allow non-Muslim visitors; hours, tickets, dress code, photography rules.
- **Context** – Short, neutral history and architecture; “why is it important” without religious doctrine.
- **Discovery** – “Most beautiful mosques,” “best mosques to visit,” “tourist-friendly mosques.”

### How we appeal
- Prominent “Tourist Friendly” filter and badge; visitor-focused copy on mosque pages (hours, dress code, tips).
- Content: “Tourist-friendly mosques,” “Masjid architecture explained,” “History of the Blue Mosque,” “Cost of the world’s largest mosques.”
- Tone: informative, neutral, inclusive; explain terms (e.g. “qibla,” “minaret”) when used.
- Avoid assumption of religious knowledge; do not dilute significance for Muslim users—add a short “For visitors” or “Visitor info” section instead.

### Example messaging
- “Explore iconic mosques open to visitors—architecture, history, and practical visiting tips.”
- “From Istanbul to Abu Dhabi: discover the world’s most stunning mosques and how to visit them.”

## 4. Students & educators

### Who they are
- Students (school or university) and teachers covering Islam, world religions, architecture, or geography.
- Need accurate, citeable facts: names, dates, capacity, area, significance; sometimes Quran/hadith in context.

### What they want
- **Accuracy** – Correct names, dates, and stats; clear distinction between fact and estimate.
- **Scope** – “Biggest by area/capacity,” “oldest,” “most significant”; comparisons and timelines.
- **Sources** – Where numbers and claims come from; last updated date.

### How we appeal
- Publish clear “Source” or “Last verified” on mosque data where possible; follow [skills.md](./skills.md) for Quran/hadith.
- Content: “Largest mosques by capacity/area,” “History of grand mosques,” “Masjid architecture explained,” timelines.
- Tone: factual, neutral, educational; avoid opinion or sectarian framing.

### Example messaging
- “Accurate, well-sourced information on the world’s major mosques—for study and travel.”
- “Compare capacity, area, and history; use our timelines and lists for projects and teaching.”

## 5. Architecture & design enthusiasts

### Who they are
- People interested in Islamic or global architecture; may search “mosque architecture,” “Ottoman mosques,” “largest dome.”

### What they want
- **Style & history** – Architectural style (Ottoman, Mughal, Moorish, modern); key features (dome, minaret, materials).
- **Comparisons** – “Biggest by area,” “most expensive,” “most iconic”; images and clear specs.
- **Stories** – Who built it, when, why; design and construction.

### How we appeal
- Expose “architectural style” and key facts (dome, minaret, area); high-quality images and alt text.
- Content: “Masjid architecture explained,” “Ottoman vs. Mughal mosque architecture,” “Cost of the biggest mosques,” “Famous mosque domes.”
- Tone: informative and appreciative; link to specific mosques in the app.

### Example messaging
- “Explore Islamic architecture through the world’s most significant mosques—styles, history, and scale.”
- “From classical Ottoman to modern marvels: domes, minarets, and the stories behind them.”

## 6. Summary: persona vs. focus

| Persona | Primary focus | Content & tone |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|
| Muslims (ummah) | Sacred sites, accuracy, spiritual value | Reverent; Quran/hadith when relevant; holy sites and history |
| Muslim travelers | Discovery by place, practical visit info | Welcoming, practical; country/visitor filters and guides |
| Tourists (non-Muslim) | Access, dress code, neutral context | Inclusive, neutral; “tourist-friendly” and visitor guides |
| Students & educators | Facts, sources, comparisons | Factual, citeable; lists, timelines, “explained” pieces |
| Architecture enthusiasts | Style, scale, history, cost | Informative; architecture and construction content |

Use these personas when writing copy, designing filters, and planning blog topics so the product and content stay relevant and respectful for each audience.
