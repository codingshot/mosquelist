## Founder / personal angle (blog & announcements)

**Use when:** Posting as founder, launch blog, “why I built this,” personal social, newsletter.

**Core anecdote:** One of my favorite things to do when traveling is to pray in the biggest mosque in the city—and to seek out the best-designed mosques. You learn so much about local Muslim populations by simply showing up: how they build, how they gather, what they value. I built MosqueList so more people could discover these places and plan their own journeys. Below are several angles you can use.

### Angle 1: Praying in the biggest mosque
**Hook:** “One of my favorite things to do when I travel is find the biggest mosque and pray there.”

- There’s something about standing in a space built for thousands—the scale, the silence before the adhan, the diversity of the congregation. I started keeping a list of the ones I’d visited, then realized others might want the same: not just names, but capacity, history, and whether you can actually visit. That’s how MosqueList began—so you can discover the world’s largest and most significant mosques and add them to your own list.

**Twitter / X (≈280):**  
One of my favorite travel habits: find the biggest mosque in the city and pray there. The scale, the congregation, the history. I built MosqueList so you can discover 199+ significant mosques—capacity, design, visitor info—and build your own list. 👉 [link]

### Angle 2: Best-designed mosques
**Hook:** “I’m obsessed with how different communities express faith through architecture.”

- From Ottoman domes to Mughal gardens to minimalist modern—every region has a vocabulary. When I travel, I seek out the mosques that stop you in your tracks: the best designed, the most distinctive. MosqueList lets you filter by architectural style, explore on a map, and read fact-checked articles on Islamic architecture so you can plan a trip around beauty as much as spirituality.

**Twitter / X (≈280):**  
I travel for mosques—the ones that make you stop and stare. Ottoman, Mughal, Persian, modern. I built MosqueList so you can explore 199+ by style, country, and visitor access—and build a bucket list of the world’s best-designed mosques. 👉 [link]

### Angle 3: Learning about Muslim populations through their mosques
**Hook:** “The quickest way to understand a Muslim community? Visit their mosque.”

- When I land in a new city, I look up the main congregational mosque. Who built it, when, and how it’s used tells you a lot about that place. I wanted a single app where you could discover mosques by country and region, see capacity and history, and learn as you travel. MosqueList is that—199+ mosques in 50+ countries, with filters, a map, and articles so you can explore Muslim heritage wherever you go.

**Twitter / X (≈280):**

## Persona 4: Partners / press / “what is MosqueList?”

**Angle:** Clear product narrative, scale, features, respect for sacred sites.

### One-liner (for journalists or partners)
“MosqueList is a free web app that helps people discover and plan visits to the world’s most significant mosques—199+ in 50+ countries—with an interactive map, personal mosque bucket list, and 35+ fact-checked articles on Islamic history, visitor etiquette, and architecture. Built with a focus on accuracy and respect for sacred sites.”

### Short paragraph (press release or partner page)
MosqueList is a free web app for discovering and planning visits to significant mosques worldwide. Users can explore 199+ mosques in 50+ countries—from the three holiest sites in Islam to architectural landmarks—filter by country, capacity, and visitor access, build a personal mosque bucket list, and view locations on an interactive map. The site includes an Islamic History timeline (622 CE to present), a glossary, and 35+ fact-checked blog articles on topics such as visitor etiquette, Islamic architecture, and the holiest sites. MosqueList is aimed at Muslims planning a spiritual journey and at travelers interested in Islamic heritage; the team emphasizes accuracy, cited sources, and respectful representation of sacred places.
