# Voice and Tone — MosqueList

Guidelines for how MosqueList should sound in marketing, social, and product copy. **Internal use only.**

---

## Voice (who we are)

- **Reverent** — We speak about sacred sites and Islamic heritage with respect. No casual or flippant language around holy places.
- **Accurate** — We use correct names (e.g. Masjid al-Haram, Al-Masjid an-Nabawi), proper attribution (e.g. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ), and avoid overclaiming or sensationalism.
- **Welcoming** — We address Muslims and non-Muslims alike: inclusive, clear, and helpful.
- **Calm and clear** — We avoid hype, ALL CAPS, or aggressive CTAs. We inform and inspire rather than push.

---

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone |
|---------|------|
| Holy sites / sacred content | More reverent; minimal casual language; no jokes about sacred places. |
| Travel / tourist-friendly | Practical, welcoming; “you can visit,” “here’s what to expect.” |
| Product features | Clear and helpful; “you can…,” “filter by…,” “track your visits.” |
| Social / inspirational | Warm and aspirational; “your journey,” “places to pray,” “discover.” |
| Press / partners | Professional and factual; focus on what the product does and for whom. |

---

## Do

- Use “mosque” and “masjid” appropriately (e.g. “Masjid al-Haram” when referring to that mosque).
- Use “holy site” / “holiest sites” with care; reserve for the three holiest (Mecca, Medina, Al-Aqsa) unless clearly qualified.
- Say “Prophet Muhammad ﷺ” (or “peace be upon him”) when referring to the Prophet.
- Use “we” for the product/team and “you” for the user.
- Keep sentences and paragraphs short; use lists for features and steps.
- Lead with benefit: “Discover…,” “Plan…,” “Track…” rather than only “We have a list of mosques.”

---

## Don’t

- Don’t use slang or memes around sacred sites or religious content.
- Don’t sensationalize (“You won’t believe…,” “Mind-blowing…”).
- Don’t proselytize to non-Muslims; inform and welcome.
- Don’t claim religious authority (e.g. we don’t issue fatwas or interpret scripture).
- Don’t use ALL CAPS for emphasis; use bold or structure instead.
- Don’t promise what we don’t deliver (e.g. “book Hajj” if we don’t offer it).
- Don’t use imagery or copy that could be seen as disrespectful to any school of thought or culture.

---

## Examples

**Good:**  
“Discover the world’s most significant mosques—from the three holiest sites to architectural landmarks. Filter by country, save favorites, and build your mosque bucket list.”

**Avoid:**  
“OMG you HAVE to see these insane mosques!!! 🔥”

**Good:**  
“Al-Masjid an-Nabawi is the second holiest site in Islam and the burial place of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Non-Muslims may not enter; visitors need a valid visa.”

**Avoid:**  
“Check out this crazy huge mosque in Medina.”

**Good:**  
“Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is open to visitors. Modest dress is required; abayas are provided for women. Photography is allowed in designated areas.”

**Avoid:**  
“Come take your best selfie at this mosque!”

---

## Religious and cultural sensitivity

- **Scripture and hadith:** If we quote, we cite and do not interpret. Prefer existing in-app usage (e.g. the hadith about “best of places are the mosques”) and keep context clear.
- **Sects and schools:** We describe mosques and history in a way that does not favor or dismiss any mainstream tradition.
- **Politics and conflict:** We describe locations and access in a factual, neutral way (e.g. “check current access” for sensitive regions) and avoid political framing.
- **Images:** Use respectful, accurate imagery; no irreverent or inappropriate use of sacred spaces.

Use this doc when reviewing ad copy, social posts, and any public-facing text so MosqueList stays consistent and trustworthy.
